Understanding image forensics

An educational introduction to image tampering, deep-learning screening, and the limits of Error Level Analysis.

Digital image tampering

How can an image be altered?

Digital image manipulation ranges from routine edits to changes that alter the meaning of a photograph. Two common forms of tampering are copy-move editing and image splicing.

Copy-move

In a copy-move edit, a region is copied from one part of an image and placed elsewhere in that same image. The operation may conceal an object or repeat a visual element; duplicated texture or compression behavior can sometimes provide clues for further inspection.

Splicing

Splicing combines content from two or more images. Differences in lighting, sharpness, noise, perspective, or compression history may occur at the boundaries between sources, but ordinary image processing can create similar inconsistencies.

A preliminary screening tool

This project combines a deep-learning classifier with an ELA visualization to help users decide whether an image merits additional review.

Its output is not a substitute for source verification, expert examination, or a complete forensic workflow. A model score is an estimate, not a certainty.

Reading the visualization

What Error Level Analysis can—and cannot—show

ELA recompresses an image as JPEG and compares that version with the original. The resulting difference image highlights areas that responded differently to recompression. Images with consistent JPEG histories may show relatively uniform differences, while an edited region can sometimes stand out.

False positives

Bright edges, text, fine textures, and areas with strong contrast can naturally produce higher residual differences. Resizing, re-saving, or converting an image can also affect the visualization; brightness alone does not establish that an image was edited.

False negatives

Repeated lossy compression can make different regions look more alike, and some edits may leave no clearly distinguishable ELA pattern. ELA can therefore fail to highlight genuine manipulation. PNG input is converted for analysis and may have different baseline compression behavior.
